# Green Light Sound — "Andy Wallace" bass EQ trick

**This is about bass, not drums.** It's kept here because the kick and the bass
share the low end, and because the idea (boost the fundamental hard *before*
a compressor) is the same one the drum notes keep returning to.

**It is secondhand.** Joe describes a technique he attributes to the mixer Andy
Wallace and says it "kind of mimics his workflow" with plugins. He notes
Wallace gives few interviews and talks about philosophy more than gear, so
treat the settings as Joe's reconstruction, not Wallace's own words.

## The technique

**Two stages: a Neve-style EQ, then an SSL channel strip.** Joe uses Waves'
Scheps 73 (a 1073 model) and says any 1073/1084/1081-style EQ will do, then an
SSL **G-series** channel strip, because Wallace works on a G-series console.

1. **Feed the DI and the bass-amp signal together into one bus.** In the
   console picture: DI and amp come off the tape into the SSL, then go out to
   the 1073 *before* any SSL EQ or compression.
2. **1073: crank the low band at the bass's fundamental.**
   - Pick the low-band frequency closest to **~40 Hz** (the low E is about
     40 Hz). The 1073's low band is **35 Hz** and that is what he uses on the
     Scheps 73.
   - Push the gain **all the way up** ("which is crazy, I know").
   - On its own it gives "tons of low end, almost out of control".
3. **SSL G-series EQ: most of the work is in the low mids.**
   - **High-pass at ~40 Hz** and roll off some top.
   - **Cut ~240 Hz by ~1.6 dB.** In the Wallace version, most of the EQ work is
     between the filters and **about 200–300 Hz**.
   - Joe's own additions: a little top-end EQ and some upper mids "just
     because I wanted them there", to let the amp character through.
4. **SSL compressor: ratio 4:1.** Threshold and release are on screen, not in
   the transcript.

**Result:** "a big powerful low end of the bass but still … a controlled
sound" because the SSL compressor holds the cranked low end.

**Next step he mentions, not shown:** automating between the DI and the amp
parts, another Wallace habit, to be covered in a later video.

## Relevance to the drum notes (inference)

**[I]** throughout.

- **Boost the note, then compress it.** The same shape as the kick
  chain in the [Wildfire note](nolly-periphery-wildfire-masterclass.md)
  (saturate, then an opto compressor) and the dynamic thump boost in the
  [overhead trick](colt-capperrune-overhead-trick.md): get the fundamental
  hot first and let the compressor hold it, instead of EQ'ing after the
  compressor. The order matters: **EQ before compression** here.
- **Mid cut next to a low boost.** Boosting ~40 Hz and cutting ~240 Hz echoes
  the kick advice (clear the zone just above the fundamental) and the
  high-Q snare shelf in the [snare tutorial](nolly-tips-eqing-any-snare.md).
- **Kick and bass share the low end.** If the bass is built like this, the kick
  has to leave room: compare the kick-triggered Trackspacer ducking of the
  bass in the Wildfire note.
- **Signal [I]:** the cranked 1073 band is a **Tone** move (a tonal EQ,
  before the compressor); the 240 Hz cut and the 40 Hz high-pass are
  corrective, **Rescue EQ**; the 4:1 SSL is **Tone** compression (stylised,
  not levelling). A cranked boost this size is also exactly where a block can
  hit the **±24 dB gain-neutral cap**, so it would need measuring.
