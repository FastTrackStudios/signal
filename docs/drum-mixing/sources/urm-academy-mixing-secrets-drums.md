# URM Academy — Mixing Secrets vol. 3: drums (article)

A blog round-up. The settings are the author's; most named engineers
(Sturgis, Weiss, CLA…) appear only as linked videos. Treat as **secondhand**
and general.

## Overheads / cymbals (EQ)

- **≤ 500 Hz:** "in metal mixes this is often fully rolled off" — but don't
  filter it by reflex.
- **2–4 kHz:** cutting harshness here makes the overheads more listenable and
  frees space for guitars and vocals.
- **500 Hz and 800 Hz:** cut if the overheads sound boxy or flat.
- **8 kHz and up:** a gentle shelf for sheen; "notoriously hard on the ears".
- **Limiting overheads** (a linked Sturgis video shows Waves L1 on an
  overhead): when elements "jump out of the drum mix unnaturally".

## Bleed

- Cymbal spot mics: remove bleed by hand (rarely played), with natural fades.
- **Multiband expansion on the snare's high band** to reduce cymbal bleed
  between snare hits.

## Rooms

- **Crush:** a UA 1176, **fast attack, fast release, 10 dB+ of gain
  reduction** — "increase the room sustain of the drum shells and reduce the
  'peakiness' of the drum transients"; **"all buttons in"** for over the top.
- Distortion on rooms (Devil-Loc), blended under the dry signal.

## Other

- Compression attack 10–30 ms lets transients through.
- Chris Lord-Alge is known for "absolutely cranking" an 8 kHz shelf on drum
  mics (secondhand).

## Signal mapping (inference)

| Move | Signal phase |
|---|---|
| OH ≤ 500 Hz roll-off (metal) | Rescue EQ — ours is HPF 150 Hz, a deliberate choice (OHs carry some kit image) |
| 2–4 kHz harshness cut | Tone EQ, or Polish (keyed dynamic band) |
| OH limiting | Tone (comp 20:1, fast) |
| 1176-style room crush 10 dB+ | Tone (thickness: fast/fast) |
| Snare high-band expansion | Rescue (dynamic EQ, keyed by snare hits) |
