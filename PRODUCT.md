# Product

## Platform

web

One custom design language everywhere, rendered through Blitz (dioxus-native:
Vello + wgpu) on macOS, iPhone and iPad, the browser remote, and inside
CLAP/VST3 plugin editors. Layouts adapt to size and touch, not to each OS's
native conventions.

## Users

Any musician who plays through a rig. The guitar rig is first and most
complete; a keys rig is in progress, and drum and other rigs follow. Today's
heaviest use is live worship: a player with the rig on a laptop or iPad at
their feet, a MIDI footswitch, a setlist for the night, and tones dialled in
beforehand at home or in rehearsal.

## Product Purpose

Signal is a complete instrument rig in software: the signal chain, the
tones, and the performance layer that switches them during a set. It exists
so a musician can play a show from one rig that sounds the same on every
device it runs on, and change sound per song without menu-diving on stage.
Success is a set played start to finish without the rig getting in the way:
no dropouts, no wrong patch, nothing to look up mid-song.

## Positioning

**A headless rig with any number of remotes.** The engine runs with no GUI
at all; every interface (the laptop window, the iPad, a browser, a plugin
editor) is a remote attached to it over the network, and they all render
the same UI. A player can play from the pedalboard, tweak from an iPad, and
watch from a laptop on one running rig. A conventional amp-sim plugin or
modeller app, where the GUI is the process, cannot make that claim.

## Operating Context

- **On stage:** dark room, glanced at from a standing distance, hands busy
  with an instrument. The rig is driven mostly by MIDI footswitches; the
  screen confirms the patch, the song, the tuner and the meters.
- **Rehearsal and prep:** seated, building profiles, patches, presets and
  setlists, and auditioning tones at length.
- **Devices:** MacBook-class laptops (the 14" MacBook Pro at 1512x945 is the
  reference size), iPad, iPhone in landscape, a desktop browser, and
  DAW plugin hosts including REAPER.
- **Rituals:** a setlist per service or gig, songs carrying their own key,
  BPM and patch changes, and a profile per band or style (e.g. Worship,
  Blues).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Vocabulary** (see `README.md` and `crates/signal/docs/DOMAIN.md`):
  Block, Module, Layer, Engine, Preset; Rig; Snapshot (variant); Profile and
  Patch; Song and Scene; Setlist. Modules in the guitar rig include Core
  (comp, gate, drives, amp, EQ) and Time (delays and reverbs).
- **Performance layer:** Preset, Profile and Setlist modes; footswitch
  grid, macros, tuner, input/output/headphone meters, global FX bypass and
  boost.
- **Detachable GUI is strict:** the rig core is fully headless; nothing the
  UI needs may live only in the UI process.
- **Rendering constraints:** inline styles only in Signal UI crates (Blitz
  does not reliably load external CSS); components must render correctly
  without Tailwind; root components take no props, so one component serves
  standalone, plugin and embedded use.
- **Realtime safety outranks the UI:** the audio thread must never xrun. A
  UI that redraws every frame (meters, visualisers) has to stay inside the
  frame budget.
- **Hardware faces:** pedals, amps and studio units are drawn as faces
  generated by the `frame` repo (`examples/plugin-faces`), in tiers per
  available space (strip, phone, pane, desktop, fullscreen).

## Product Principles

1. **The rig never depends on the screen.** Anything you can do from a
   remote you can also do with no remote attached; the UI reflects the rig's
   state, it never owns it.
2. **Stage first.** What a player needs mid-song (current patch, next song,
   tuner, levels, switches) is readable at a glance from standing distance.
   Editing depth can live a click away.
3. **One rig, every device.** The same UI adapts to laptop, tablet, phone
   and plugin window by size and input, never by forking into separate
   designs.
4. **Build once, reuse everywhere.** Tones are composed from shared parts
   (modules, block presets, presets) so a sound made for one song or profile
   is available to all of them.
5. **Instrument-agnostic.** Guitar is first, not special; vocabulary and
   layout must hold for keys, drums and future rigs.
